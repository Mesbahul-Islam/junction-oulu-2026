# Who pays, and why everyone gains

Reading is free. Organisations that need people in Oulu to be well informed pay Kaleva, ads fill the rest, and part of that money comes back to the readers who help as rewards. Readers who don't want ads can pay a small flat fee to remove them.

## The problem with a reader subscription

An international student or engineer in Oulu will not pay €5 a month to read local news in English. They paste a Kaleva article into Google Translate or ask in a WhatsApp group. The information they need exists; it just isn't reaching them in a language they read.

Many organisations lose money when it doesn't reach them:

- A university loses tuition and reputation when international students drop out or leave after a lonely first year.
- An employer that relocated an engineer loses the cost of hiring and moving them if that person leaves within a year.
- The city wants new residents to stay, pay taxes and use services correctly.
- Local businesses can't reach a group of thousands of residents who don't read Finnish ads.

So the customer is the organisation, and the reader is who it is paying for.

## How Kaleva makes money

### 1. Organisation access (main income)

A university, employer or the city pays Kaleva a yearly licence. In return:

- Their people join with an organisation code and use the app free, labelled "provided by [organisation]".
- They can add their own notices (orientation week, a change to campus bus routes) to the same feed, clearly marked as coming from them, not Kaleva's newsroom.
- They get a monthly report with counts only: how many of their people are reading, in which languages, and which topics. No names and no reading history.

Price would scale with the number of people covered. A department, a whole university and the city would be different tiers.

### 2. Ads for people who don't read Finnish

Job openings in English, rental flats, language courses, shops and services. This is a group Kaleva's Finnish print and web ads don't reach today, so it is new ad space rather than ads moved from one place to another. Ads are always marked as ads and are kept apart from news.

**No ads for €1 a month or €10 a year.** Anyone can take it, contributors included, and it doesn't change their level or discounts. The yearly price gives two months free and steers people to one payment a year, so card and app store fees don't eat most of a €1 charge every month. Everything else stays the same: the news, the community and the rewards are free either way. People covered by an organisation licence can be ad-free as part of that licence.

| Readers paying for no ads (yearly) | Per year |
|---|---|
| 5,000 | €50,000 |
| 20,000 | €200,000 |
| 50,000 | €500,000 |

### 3. Partner discounts

Local businesses offer discounts to Kaleva's contributors, the way Revolut shows partner rewards to its members. Each partner sets a discount for every contributor level, and a member gets one code per partner each month:

| Partner | Discount on | Contributor | Local guide | Trusted voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hesburger | meals | 15% | 25% | 40% |
| DNA | mobile and broadband, first 6 months | 10% | 20% | 30% |
| Tokmanni | home and everyday items | 5% | 10% | 15% |
| Prisma | groceries | 5% | 8% | 10% |
| PowerPark | day passes | | 25% | 50% |
| Finnair | flights within Finland | | 10% | 20% |

The business gives the discount, not cash, and pays Kaleva a placement fee to be in front of an active, local audience it can't reach through Finnish ads. It's cheap for them, since a discounted meal costs Hesburger little and brings in a new regular customer, and Kaleva pays nothing towards the rewards.

### 4. More readers for Kaleva itself

Every translated story links to Kaleva's original. Readers who arrive for the translated version are new traffic, and some of them will later read Kaleva in Finnish as they learn it.

## How readers gain

- The app is free, with ads, or ad-free for €1 a month or €10 a year. They read local news in their own language, with a short explanation when a story depends on Finnish context ("what this means if you are on a student residence permit").
- Their own community's news (international students, a neighbourhood, a language group) shows up next to Kaleva's.
- People who contribute get something back (next section).

## Why anyone would contribute

People already post useful updates in Facebook and WhatsApp groups for free. They do it to help friends, to be known as someone who knows things, and to stop wrong information spreading. The app keeps those reasons and adds a reward.

1. **Recognition.** A member whose posts keep being approved by editors builds a trust score and becomes a Trusted voice, with a badge on their posts. An editor has to approve the step, so the badge can't be gamed. (Built.)
2. **Rewards for approved work only.** A post, photo or correction that an editor approves earns points. Rejected posts earn nothing. Points raise your level (Reader, Contributor, Local guide, Trusted voice) and each level raises the discount at every partner. A member taps a partner and gets a code to show at the till, once a month per partner. No cash is ever paid. This is deliberate: paying for every tip would reward people for making things up. (Built.)
3. **Protecting each other.** When a rumour spreads ("Migri is cancelling permits for students who work over 30 hours"), a member can send it in to be checked. The answer links to the official source, such as Migri or the city. On permits, healthcare and tax, the community never decides what is true by voting.

## How the newsroom stays independent

- Organisations pay for access for their people, not for coverage. A university that pays gets no say over what Kaleva writes about it.
- Organisation notices and ads are always labelled and never look like journalism.
- An editor approves community posts before they are shown as verified, in line with the Finnish journalist guidelines (JSN). Until then they are marked as community posts.
- Faces in community photos are blurred automatically on upload, and a moderator checks the result before the photo goes live. (Built.)

## Who pays and who gains

| Who | Pays | Gets |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Nothing, or €1 a month / €10 a year for no ads | Local news in their language, their community's news, rewards for helping |
| Contributor | Their time | Trust score, badge, rewards for approved posts |
| University, employer, city | Yearly licence | Better-informed people who are more likely to stay, a channel for their notices, usage report |
| Local business | Ads, or a placement fee plus the discount itself | Reach to residents who don't read Finnish |
| Kaleva | Translation and moderation costs | Licence and ad income, new readers, community news it wouldn't otherwise have |
