The Challenge
BACKGROUND –––––––––––– Local media reaches few of the residents and workers in our region whose first language is not Finnish. Their number is growing across Kaleva Media’s coverage area, and their information needs vary widely.

EXAMPLES OF COMMUNITIES –––––––––––––––––––––––––

Work-based immigrants and international professionals and their families, for example in Oulu’s tech sector.
International students and researchers at universities and universities of applied sciences.
Seasonal tourism workers in Lapland, who arrive for the winter season at ski resorts and tourist destinations. They quickly need information on working conditions, housing, healthcare, transport, safety and everyday local life, often for just a few months.
Residents with a refugee background and others who have settled permanently, whose first language may be, for example, Arabic, Ukrainian, Russian or Somali.
Finland’s own language minorities: Swedish speakers and Sámi speakers, whose languages in Finland are North Sámi, Inari Sámi and Skolt Sámi. There is little news content in the Sámi languages, and the vitality of these languages also depends on their being used in everyday life. Information is currently scattered across official websites, Facebook groups and WhatsApp chains. Some of it is outdated or wrong. Each of these communities already has its own networks, knowledge and trusted voices. AI agents can help monitor sources, identify topics that matter, and translate and explain them at a scale no traditional newsroom could manage, but the medium only works if the community itself is part of it.
THE CHALLENGE –––––––––––––– Design and prototype a new multilingual news medium built with and for a community. The newsroom is run by agents that find, select, produce and distribute content, but the community is at its heart: it helps decide what matters, contributes stories and local knowledge, and holds the medium accountable. Choose one or more communities and justify your choice.

GROUND RULES –––––––––––––––

The agentic newsroom must follow the Journalist’s Guidelines of the Council for Mass Media in Finland: https://jsn.fi/en/journalists-guidelines/
A human must always be in the loop. We only do responsible journalism.
YOUR CONCEPT COULD INCLUE, for example: ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––

Monitoring agents that follow city council decisions, official announcements, events and local news, and identify what matters to a specific community.
Explanatory journalism instead of plain translation: what does this decision mean for me if I’ve just moved to Finland, or come to Levi to work for the season? Readers may bring assumptions from their own culture that the original story never thought to spell out, so explanatory context may need to come from outside the story itself.
Community participation: members can ask questions, suggest stories, share local knowledge, correct mistakes and take part in deciding what gets covered.
Channels where communities already are: messaging apps, audio, short-form video or local community groups.
A bridge in the other direction: stories about these communities for Finnish-speaking audiences.
Supporting languages: for example, a Sámi-language local news feed that brings the language into everyday use.
THINGS TO CONSIDER –––––––––––––––

Community: how is the community involved in building, running and evaluating the medium, not just as an audience? Who are its trusted voices, and how do they take part?
Distribution: in what form does the end product reach people? An app, or something else entirely? Justify your choice from the community’s point of view.
Human in the loop: how do the agents share the work, and where and how does a human stay in the loop? An agentic newsroom should be based on how a real newsroom works. This article explains the roles, hierarchy and workflow of a typical newsroom: https://journalism.university/reporting-techniques/functions-newsroom-journalism-heart. The agents’ choices can be guided by Kaleva Media’s user needs model, in which every story serves at least one reader need: Information (what happened), Understanding (why, and what it means), Usefulness (how it helps me in everyday life), Emotion (moves or inspires), Break (entertains and offers a breather) and Community (connects people and gives their community a voice).
Quality and errors: how do you prevent translation and explanation errors that could affect, for example, residence permits, health or employment?
Trust: why would a community trust this more than its own Facebook group? How is the role of AI disclosed openly?
Language choices: which languages and communities come first, and how do you justify the choice?
Small languages: AI translation quality for the Sámi languages is weaker than for major languages, and errors can offend the language community. How are humans and the community involved in quality assurance?
Short life cycle: a seasonal worker needs information on arrival and only for a few months. How do you reach them in time?
Cultural context: the same news story is not equally important or understandable to everyone.
Scalability: does the model work in smaller towns and for less common languages?
BUSINESS MODEL ––––––––––––––– Good journalism can’t survive without revenue. We want to see that teams have thought about money as seriously as technology.

Willingness to pay: do the target audiences want to, and can they, pay for news? Does this differ between groups, for example a tech professional and a seasonal worker? How could you test the assumption?
Who benefits? Who gains financially when new residents and workers are well informed? For example, employers who want to retain talent, tourism companies that recruit seasonal workers every year, educational institutions, municipalities and service providers.
Where could the money come from? Consumer subscriptions, freemium, B2B licences for employers or educational institutions, partnerships, targeted advertising, recruitment ads, events, public funding or foundations. Or a combination?
Language minorities: for Sámi- and Swedish-language content, the funding model may be completely different from that for immigrant communities. What could it be?
Conflicts of interest: if the payer is an employer or a municipality, how is journalistic independence maintained? Can the medium report critically on an organisation that funds its existence?
Link to Kaleva Media’s business: does the concept bring new subscribers, open up advertising inventory or create an entirely new revenue stream?
Costs: an agentic newsroom is cheaper than a traditional one, but not free. What does it cost to maintain one language or community, and at what point does the model pay off?

WHAT WE VALUE IN SOLUTIONS ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––

Community at the core: is the community a genuine participant, and would this actually reach people who are currently left out of local news?
Responsible journalism: does the concept follow the Journalist’s Guidelines, with a human meaningfully in the loop?
Viability of the agentic operating model and its distribution channel
Reliability of the content and error management, including for small languages
Credibility of the business model: is the payer identified, is willingness to pay justified, and does the model hold up from the perspective of independence?
Feasibility of the prototype and quality of the demo
